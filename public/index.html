Threat Intelligence

What Happens on Unencrypted Networks

Understanding how surveillance operates is the first step toward neutralizing it. Here is how modern networks harvest user activity:

ISP Browsing Logs

In many regions, internet service providers legally log every domain query (DNS lookup) you make, building behavioral profiles sold to ad networks.

Public Wi-Fi Snooping

Open Wi-Fi at airports, hotels, and cafes allows threat actors on the same local subnet to perform packet inspection and man-in-the-middle attacks.

DNS & WebRTC Leaks

Even when using private browsing windows or standard proxies, browsers often leak real IP coordinates through WebRTC requests and system DNS fallbacks.

Data Broker Dossiers

Ad exchanges cross-reference IP addresses with device fingerprints to track your physical movement and purchasing patterns across all connected devices.

Digital Privacy & VPN Essentials

Clear, technical answers to the most common questions regarding network protection.

Even when websites use HTTPS (which encrypts the specific page content and passwords), your internet service provider can still see the domain names of every website you visit, the timestamps, and the volume of data exchanged. With a verified VPN like NordVPN or Proton VPN, all traffic from your device is encrypted in a secure tunnel, meaning your ISP sees only random encrypted data addressed to the VPN server.
Switzerland is renowned for having some of the strongest privacy legislation in the world. It is outside the EU and US intelligence agreements (such as the 5-Eyes, 9-Eyes, and 14-Eyes surveillance alliances). Under Swiss law, VPN services cannot be compelled to log user activity or execute mass surveillance directives without a specific Swiss federal court order.
NordLynx is built around the modern WireGuard protocol, which contains just ~4,000 lines of code compared to OpenVPN's 70,000+ lines. NordVPN implemented a custom double Network Address Translation (NAT) system that combines WireGuard's extreme speed and lightweight cryptography with zero identifiable IP logging on the server.

Free VPN services typically monetize by injecting advertisements, throttling connection speeds, or collecting and selling user browsing data to third-party brokers.
